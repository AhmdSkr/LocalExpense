# The Khourys of Zahlé: Three Years of Household Money

*A fictional family, 1 October 2023 to 30 September 2026. All amounts are US dollars, because in the Khoury home the dollar is the real money and the lira is just what the bakery sometimes hands back as change.*

## The cast

- **Elias Khoury (46)**, history and civics teacher at a private school, who also tutors in the evenings. Base salary $950 a month, raised to $1,000 in September 2024, plus about $100 to $200 of tutoring on a good month.
- **Rima Khoury (42)**, pharmacy assistant at a neighborhood pharmacy near Zahlé's Berdawni river. $600 a month, paid on the 29th when the owner remembers.
- **Joe (19)**, their eldest, starting engineering at a university in Beirut. He commutes by service taxi and has opinions about every driver.
- **Layla (12)**, sharp, stubborn and fond of drawing, in private school with a tutor for math.
- **Teta Marta (77)**, Elias's mother, who lives in the back bedroom with her rosary, her radio and a $90 monthly pension.
- **Tala**, who arrives in March 2025 and changes the budget, the sleeping schedule and everyone's heart.
- **Tony**, Elias's younger brother, an engineer in Montreal. He sends $250 on the twelfth of every month without being asked, and a good deal more when life gets hard.
- **Aunt Hanan**, Rima's widowed aunt in the village, who receives $60 a month and pretends not to need it.
- **Bruno**, the puppy.

They rent a third-floor apartment for $300 a month. Like every household on the street, they have three sources of power: the state electricity (EDL, when it comes), the neighborhood generator subscription (the *moteur*), and a flashlight on the hallway shelf.

## Year one: October 2023 to September 2024

**Autumn.** The year opens with Joe's university registration, $650 in one payment, paid at the cashier's window while Elias pretended the number did not hurt. The household has about $6,000 in savings, which Rima calls "the cushion" and Elias calls "the plan". Water arrives by tanker every few weeks. The generator costs about $70 in a calm month and a lot more when the cuts are long. The internet is $30 a month and everyone fights over it.

**Winter.** Teta Marta's heater breaks in December and Elias pays for the repair himself, out of pride. On 24 December the house is full: Tony's video call, a table of kibbeh and fattoush, and $310 of presents and food. In February a stray puppy follows Layla home from school. Bruno costs $180 in vaccines, a collar and a bed Teta says is too fancy for a dog.

**Spring.** Easter lunch at a cousin's house, a lean few weeks of shopping carefully, then the invitation: cousin Maya is getting married in June. On 8 June Rima buys a dress and shoes and Elias a suit he will wear for a decade. Together with the gift envelope the wedding costs $420, and the dancing goes on until the generator in the hall runs out of diesel.

**Summer.** The big decision of the year is the car. The old one, a rust-colored sedan, finally died. After weeks of cousins' advice, Elias takes $2,200 from savings on 10 July and on 12 July pays $4,600 for a used Kia Rio. In August the family spends a week in Batroun on the coast: a rented room, fish at the harbor, ice cream, sunburn, $720 in total. In early September come the school fees, uniforms and books for Layla at $980. Joe's second-year installment lands in the same weeks.

## Year two: October 2024 to September 2025

**Autumn.** On 19 October, on the road down to the coast highway, a truck clips the Kia's back corner at a roundabout. Nobody is hurt. The repairs cost $1,350. Three weeks later, on 9 November, Rima's phone is stolen from her bag in a crowded market, and the family has to replace it for $310. Money is tight and tempers shorter. On 5 December the insurance finally pays $520 toward the accident, which Elias frames and then takes to the bank.

**Winter, the lean months.** In January the school tells its teachers that salaries will be paid in installments for a while. On 28 January Elias receives $400 instead of $1,000. Rima's pharmacy salary and Tony's transfer carry the house. January is the leanest month: no restaurants, no new clothes, the generator is reduced to a smaller amperage, and Layla's tutor is paid late with an apology. From December to February not a dollar goes into the cushion.

**Spring.** Rima is expecting. Tala is born on 22 March in a private hospital in Zahlé. The delivery, three nights of care and the pediatrician come to $2,400, paid in cash before discharge, with Tony wiring $1,000 that week. It is the heaviest month since the car. Friends bring cake, diapers, gold chains and Arabic blessings. Rima takes a few months of reduced hours.

**Summer.** On 20 May a call from the village: a plot of land that Elias and his siblings inherited, long considered worthless, has sold. His share is $3,500. After the shock, he deposits it and says nothing for a week. On 15 June Tala is baptized in the village church, followed by a lunch for forty people, the priest's offering, a photographer and the white outfit: $650. On 2 August the family drives up to the Cedars for a long weekend with Teta in the back seat, lungs full of mountain air, $540. In September, Joe's third-year fees and Layla's return to school take another bite.

## Year three: October 2025 to September 2026

**Autumn.** Rima's brother Samir lost his shop and needs help to pay his rent. The Khourys send $400, and the subject is never raised again. On 14 November Teta Marta, who has been weaker since the cold came, is taken to hospital with pneumonia. Eight days, medicine and tests cost $1,600. She comes home thinner but laughing.

**Winter.** She does not make it through the January cold. Teta Marta dies on 18 January. The funeral on 20 January, with the church, the flowers, the obituary notices, the reception and the meal for mourners, costs $1,250, and half the village comes. Elias goes back to class on Monday with red eyes. The house is quiet.

**Spring.** The generator bills have always irritated Elias, and the shock of the last two years has made him decisive. On 12 April he pays $3,800 for a small solar system with a battery, a loan from savings and the windfall's remains. The next generator bill is a third of its former size. In May the electrician drinks coffee on the balcony and tells them they will pay it back in two years.

**Summer.** On 27 June, Joe graduates. The ceremony, the gown, the photographer and the dinner come to $350, and Layla draws a cartoon of him in a mortarboard that ends up on the fridge. In August Joe starts at a small engineering firm. Elias's school is paying full salaries again. In September, Tala takes her first steps and Bruno, now fully grown, chases her across the kitchen floor.

## What the numbers show

Over three years the Khourys earn a little more than they spend, but only because of the windfall, Tony's quiet loyalty and the savings that they drew down and refilled. The biggest lesson is the one every family on the street knows: a household budget here is mostly a list of things that were not supposed to happen.
